# Sharing with family

The whole of it is one button. Wherever you are — a book, a photo, a person in
the family tree — **Send to** takes what you're looking at and gets it to
somewhere: a member of the household, your own e-reader, or anyone at all
through a link.

There is nothing to set up, and nothing new to learn per page. The button sits
in the same place on everything.

## Sending something to someone

Open the thing, press **Send to**, pick a name, and add a line if you want to.
That's it.

What they get is a **pointer, not a copy**. Nothing is emailed, nothing is
duplicated, and no file leaves the server. They open it in their own account,
with their own place in it and their own progress.

If you type nothing, it still sends — the recommendation itself is the message.

### Somebody who can't open it yet

The list of names has two parts. People who can already open the thing are at
the top. Underneath, headed **Doesn't have access yet**, are the people who
can't — because it lives in a library they've never been given.

Picking one of them does both jobs at once: the button reads **Give access and
send**, and it means what it says. They get read access to that one item, and
they get told about it, from a single press.

If you only have viewing rights on the library yourself, you'll still see who's
missing — the names are just greyed out, marked **no access**. Someone who
manages that library has to be the one to widen it.

## Your e-reader

On a book, **Send to** also offers **My e-reader**, which mails the file to your
Kindle or Kobo. It only appears once your device address is saved in
[Profile → Devices](your-account.md#send-to-e-reader) and the server has an
outgoing mail server ([guide](email.md)); until then the row reads *Set up my
e-reader* and takes you there.

You can't send a file to somebody else's Kindle, on purpose. Mailing a file to
another person's device without asking isn't yours to decide — so it lands in
their **Shared with me** instead, and they send it to their own device if they
want it.

## Anyone with a link

The last destination makes a guest link: no account needed, expires on a date
you choose, revocable at any time. This is the one to use for people outside the
household. See [Sharing](../sharing.md) for the details.

## Shared with me

Everything other people have put in front of you lands on one page:
**Shared with me**, in the menu with your name on it.

Anything still waiting on a decision sits at the top under **Waiting for you**,
showing who sent it and what they said, with two buttons:

- **Save** puts it in your list, to get to when you get to it.
- **Not now** clears it away. Nothing is deleted and nobody is told.

Everything below that is simply what you can open — things people have given you
access to, including shared photo albums.

### The dot

When something new arrives, a small dot appears on your **Profile** button. It
means *there's something here you haven't looked at* — nothing more. Opening the
page clears it, whether or not you've decided about anything on it.

There is no unread count, and nothing nags. A recommendation you never get to
just sits there quietly.

## Being told by email

By default, none of this sends any email; it all happens inside the app. An
administrator can switch on a notification under
**Control panel → Settings → Notifications**, which needs an outgoing mail server
first ([guide](email.md)). Even then, the message only says who sent what and
links back to the app — it never carries the file.
